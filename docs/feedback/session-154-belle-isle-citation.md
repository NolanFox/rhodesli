This is the architectural signature of an early-20th-century glass-and-iron palm-house. There were a handful of major palm-house conservatories in the eastern US in this era — New York Botanical Garden (1902), Phipps Conservatory Pittsburgh (1893), Garfield Park Conservatory Chicago (1908), Buffalo Botanical Gardens (1900), and Belle Isle Detroit (1904). The biographical evidence (Albert Fox's RESI Detroit 1917 and 1917-1918 single, EVEN 7 Jun 1918 draft induction, EVEN 5 Jul 1918 NY Camp Devens) localizes the only-Detroit-pre-induction window to 1917-mid-1918.

## Top archival matches found

### 1. Library of Congress — Detroit Publishing Co. interior photograph (c.1905)

- **URL**: https://www.loc.gov/item/2016811684/
- **Title**: "Conservatory on Belle Isle [Park], interior, Detroit, Mich."
- **Date**: 1905 (publication c.1900-1910, consistent across LoC catalog)
- **Reproduction number / accession**: LC-DIG-det-4a17798 (digital file from original)
- **Collection**: Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division, **Detroit Publishing Company Collection**
- **Description**: Interior view of the Belle Isle Conservatory showing the same wooden-frame glass-roofed palm-house structure that existed continuously from 1904 until the 1953-54 aluminum reconstruction.
- **Architecture-match score**: **5/5** for venue identification (this is the canonical primary source for what this conservatory's interior looked like in 1904-1953); cannot establish frame-match because our photos show different angles + people, not an empty venue.
- **Rights**: No known restrictions on publication.

### 2. Library of Congress — Horticultural Building interior (between 1900-1910)

- **URL**: https://www.loc.gov/item/2016809951/
- **Title**: "[Interior, Horticultural Building, Belle Isle Park, Detroit, Mich.]"
- **Date**: between 1900 and 1910
- **Collection**: Library of Congress, Detroit Publishing Company Collection
- **Description**: Companion piece to the 1905 conservatory interior photograph; shows the related Horticultural Building (often confused with the palm-house but a distinct structure).
- **Architecture-match score**: **3/5** — same era and same campus but different building. Useful for ruling out architectural confusion but not a frame-match.

### 3. Detroit Historical Society encyclopedia — 1912 interior postcard

- **URL**: https://detroithistorical.org/learn/encyclopedia-of-detroit/anna-scripps-whitcomb-conservatory
- **Date**: 1912 (per the page's caption)
- **Collection**: Detroit Historical Society (an interior postcard included in the encyclopedia entry; no separate accession URL surfaced from the webfetch)
- **Description**: Period postcard showing the conservatory interior closest in date to our 1917-1918 window (5 years prior).
- **Architecture-match score**: **4/5** for venue identification — the 1912 postcard would predate our photos by only ~5 years, well within the unchanged-architecture window (no major reconstruction occurred until 1953).

### 4. Historic Detroit — Anna Scripps Whitcomb Conservatory old-interior gallery

- **URL**: https://historicdetroit.org/galleries/anna-scripps-whitcomb-interior-old-photos
- **Earliest dated photo**: 1927 (an interior view in the Vance Patrick collection captioned "entrance between the Aquarium and Conservatory, which was later sealed off")
- **Collection**: Vance Patrick collection (private collector, photos used by Historic Detroit under publication agreement)
- **Architecture-match score**: **3/5** — the 1927 photo is 9-10 years post-our-window; architecture is the same but framing/angles don't match. The 1944, 1948, 1950 photos are all post-our-window, same architecture.

### 5. Wikipedia / encyclopedic descriptions of original architecture

- **URL**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_Isle_Conservatory
- **Key facts triangulated against multiple sources** (Wikipedia, Detroit Historical Society, Historic Detroit, Detroit Photography):
  - Conservatory designed by **Mason & Kahn** (firm including Albert Kahn) and built **1902-1904**, opened **18 August 1904**.
  - Original construction: **wooden frame + glass**, with an **85ft (26m) curved-glass central dome** containing the palm house.
  - Layout: 5 climate-controlled sections — Palm House (central dome), Tropical House (South Wing), Cactus House and Fernery (North Wing), Show House (East Wing).
  - The original wooden-frame curved-glass structure existed unchanged from 1904 until the **1953-54 reconstruction** that replaced the wooden frame with aluminum/steel.
- **Architecture-match score**: **5/5** for venue identification — every architectural detail visible in 02068 (curved arches, palm trees, glass roof, wooden framing) is consistent with this 1904-1953 palm-house design.

- This is a known publication-density gap (1911-1925) for the venue.
